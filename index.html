Advice for Patients During a Medicines Shortage

Information and guidance for patients and carers in England

We understand that it can be concerning when a medicine you normally receive is temporarily unavailable. Medicines shortages occasionally occur in the UK and can affect community pharmacies, hospitals and dispensing practices.

The NHS, manufacturers, wholesalers and pharmacies work together to minimise disruption to patient care. This page provides practical advice about what to do if your medicine is affected by a shortage.

What should I do?

  • Order repeat prescriptions in good time before you run out of medication.
  • Speak to your pharmacist early if you are having difficulty obtaining a medicine.
  • Do not stockpile medicines or order extra supplies unnecessarily.
  • Follow advice provided by your pharmacist, GP or specialist.
  • Do not stop prescribed medication unless advised by a healthcare professional.

Why do medicines shortages occur?

Medicines shortages can occur for several reasons, including:

  • Manufacturing problems.
  • Quality assurance issues.
  • Shortages of raw ingredients.
  • Unexpected increases in demand.
  • Global supply chain disruptions.
  • Distribution and transport difficulties.

Most shortages are temporary and healthcare professionals receive regular updates regarding national supply issues and available alternatives.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if my medicine is unavailable?

Your pharmacist or prescriber may be able to:

  • Source stock from another supplier.
  • Direct you to another pharmacy.
  • Supply an alternative strength or formulation.
  • Arrange an alternative medicine if appropriate.

Can pharmacies see stock held by other pharmacies?

Usually not. Different pharmacy organisations often use separate computer systems. Pharmacy teams may sometimes contact nearby pharmacies to help locate stock.

Will I need a new prescription for an alternative medicine?

Sometimes. If an alternative treatment is needed, a GP or other authorised prescriber may need to issue a new prescription.

What if the alternative medicine causes side effects?

If you have concerns about an alternative medicine, speak with your pharmacist, GP, specialist team or NHS 111.

What if there is no suitable alternative?

In rare circumstances there may be no suitable alternative available. Your healthcare team will discuss the available options and agree a management plan with you.

Will my pharmacist know when supplies return?

Pharmacists receive regular supply updates and may be able to advise when medicines are expected to become available again.

What is being done nationally to manage medicines shortages?

Manufacturers, wholesalers, regulators and NHS organisations work together to identify shortages early and minimise their impact on patients.

Need further advice?

If you have concerns regarding your medicines, please contact:

  • Your community pharmacy
  • Your GP practice
  • Your hospital specialist team (if applicable)
  • NHS 111 for urgent advice when your usual services are unavailable

This information provides general guidance only. Advice from your doctor, pharmacist or other healthcare professional should always take precedence.
